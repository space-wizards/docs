# Races and Species
One aspect which sets Space Station 14 apart is that players are free to choose from a number of distinct species to play as. Each playable species differs slightly in its biology and abilities - for example, Moths have wings and can eat fabrics - but any racial differences should be restricted to direct consequences of differing biology. There are no "racial bonuses" in Space Station - species should not have any particular advantage or disadvantage in any particular role. Especially odious is the idea of differing intelligence between species - any species that is pitched as "Strong but stupid" or "Weak but smart" should be rejected out of hand. All player species should be written to be equally capable of intellectual acts, and should never be implied to predispose to any particular profession or worldview.

In canon, each of the main, playable species in Space Station 14 is known and familiar to all player characters. Racial conflict and xenophobia are not components of Space Station lore. Likewise, there are no "good" or "evil" races - the ranks of all main factions, including antagonists, are filled with members of every species.

In addition, as part of the ambiguity pillar, species "lore" like homeworlds, traditions, etc. should be left largely unstated, with the notable exception of suggested naming schemes. Each species has an associated naming scheme assigned to it, which outside of graphical and audio design is one of the only means that designers have to suggest how players might choose to roleplay a given species. For example, the plantlike Diona have flowery names like "The Meadows of Excellence" or "The Apiary of Distraught." While individual players have no obligation to adhere to their species' naming scheme, players can and will still use it to make inferences about what their species' broader culture might be like and, thus, how their character might behave.
